# When there's no case study yet

This method needs one true sentence: *"I know how to do Y because I already did Z, and here are the results."* Without a Z, everything downstream is an assertion, and founders discount assertions instantly.

If the user has no Z, the fastest route to a great role is not better outreach. It's three to six weeks spent manufacturing one real, provable result. Say that directly — it's the more useful answer, even though it's not the one they asked for.

## What counts as evidence

It does not need to be prestigious. It needs to be **real, attributable, and numeric**.

Counts:
- A side project with actual users or revenue, however small
- Unpaid work for a friend's business that moved a number
- A public teardown or analysis that a practitioner endorsed or acted on
- An open-source tool with real adoption
- An audience built in a specific niche
- Someone credible who will say on the record: they did this, it worked

Doesn't count: courses completed, frameworks learned, things they'd be good at, hypothetical plans, a CV with three years of unspecified responsibility.

## Fast paths to a Z

Ordered by speed to a defensible number.

**1. The free-but-scoped favour (1–3 weeks).** Find a small business in the target market — a friend's, a local one, anyone with a product and a visible gap. Run the exact play they intend to sell, tightly scoped, and measure it. This is the single fastest legitimate case study, and its main cost is asking.

**2. The public teardown (days).** Take five companies in the target market, analyze the gap the user specializes in, publish what they'd do and what the evidence from competitors suggests. This isn't proof of execution, but it's proof of judgment, and it doubles as outreach — founders in that market often find it and reply first.

**3. The build (2–6 weeks).** Ship the smallest version of the thing and get real users. Works well for anything product, technical, or content-shaped; a small number with real users beats a large number of hypothetical ones.

**4. Mine work they've already done but never quantified (hours).** Very often a Z already exists and was never measured. Old projects, old accounts, old jobs — go back and find the number. Ask an ex-colleague what actually changed after the user's work. Many people discover their case study rather than build it; check here before spending weeks.

**5. The paid small job (1–2 weeks).** A small freelance engagement in the target domain, taken specifically to generate a citable outcome. Lower leverage than the others, but it pays and it's fast.

## Run it as a sprint

Make it concrete or it won't happen. Produce with the user:

- The single number they're trying to produce
- The path they're taking, and the specific person or business they're asking
- The window, with dates
- What the resulting sentence will say — write the target sentence *now*, in advance. It clarifies whether the effort will actually produce usable evidence.

Then get back to move 2 with a real Z in hand.

## Meanwhile

Positioning and target research don't need to wait. Build the list, get into the communities, start replying usefully to the founders' public posts. By the time the case study exists, the access is warm and the first messages write themselves.

## The honest version

If the user wants the shortcut — outreach now, evidence later — tell them what happens: the call goes fine, the founder asks what they've done, and the answer is thin. That founder is then permanently spent, and they were probably one of the twenty people in the world worth talking to about this market. Three weeks of evidence-building is cheaper than one burned relationship.
