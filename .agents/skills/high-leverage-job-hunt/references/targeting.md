# Move 2 — Who needs this edge

A skill isn't valuable in the abstract; it's valuable to a company where it moves a number that matters. Someone exceptional at building viral consumer products is a huge asset to a consumer startup and close to irrelevant to a high-frequency trading firm. The skill didn't change. The room did.

So this move is purely about finding the right rooms.

## The chain

Walk it in order. Each step narrows the next.

1. **Edge** — the validated thing from move 1.
2. **Result** — what it produces inside a company. Not the activity; the outcome. "Short-form content" is an activity. "A repeatable channel that delivers signups at under $2" is a result.
3. **Money path** — how that result becomes revenue or saved cost. Write it as arithmetic, even rough: more installs → more trial starts → more subscriptions → LTV × conversion.
4. **Where it's worth the most** — business models where that number is the constraint. The same result is worth 10× more to a company with high LTV and a leaky top of funnel than to one with a strong funnel and no monetization.
5. **Companies that need it and aren't doing it well** — the actual list.

Step 5 is where most people get lazy. The point isn't companies that *could* use the edge. It's companies where the gap is visible, the market has already proven the play works, and nobody inside has owned it.

## Finding the visible gap

Research each candidate company until you can name a specific missing Y. Things that reliably reveal gaps:

- **What competitors do that they don't.** Check 5–10 competitors for the play in question. If most of the market runs it and this company doesn't, that's the gap.
- **Channels present but dead.** An account with 200 followers and nothing posted for eight months. A blog that stopped in 2024. A referral program with no incentive.
- **The funnel, walked as a user.** Sign up. Where does it get confusing, slow, or silent? Does an onboarding email ever arrive?
- **Hiring signals.** An open role that's been listed for months says they know about the gap and can't fill it — excellent. A role they just filled says the gap is closed; drop it.
- **What the founder says publicly.** Podcasts, X threads, launch posts. Founders state their constraints out loud more often than people expect.
- **Recent funding.** Fresh money plus a growth mandate plus a small team equals unowned problems.

Use web search for this and check recency. Claiming a company is missing something they shipped last month ends the conversation before it starts. Verify before it goes in the list.

## Qualification scorecard

Score each company 0–2 and keep the ones that clear roughly 8. This keeps the user from spending their best outreach on companies that can't say yes.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gap is visible and specific | can't name it | vague | named, verified |
| Proof the play works in this market | none | adjacent | 2+ direct competitors doing it |
| User's evidence maps to it | unrelated | loosely | did nearly this before |
| Revenue impact if it works | marginal | meaningful | material to the plan |
| Founder is reachable | no path | cold only | warm intro available |
| Company can move | big, slow, layered | mid | founder decides today |
| Nobody owns the problem | dedicated team | someone part-time | clearly unowned |

Already-known companies with real traction are usually better targets than raw early-stage startups: distribution and product exist, so an edge applied to them produces measurable results fast — which is exactly what the user needs to build their case. Very large corporations are the weakest fit; the founder-reaches-decision-in-one-call mechanism doesn't exist there.

## The research checklist, per company

Fifteen focused minutes is enough for a first pass.

- What they sell, to whom, and how they make money
- Stage, team size, last raise
- The specific missing Y, with the evidence trail
- 2–3 named competitors running Y, and anything public about how it's working for them
- A rough impact estimate — state the assumption, don't fake precision: *"they're at maybe 2k signups/month; this channel typically adds 20–40% for products like this"*
- Founder name, what they care about publicly, and the route to them
- Anything the user already has in common with them: shared market, shared connection, shared prior employer

## Output

The target table from SKILL.md, ten well-researched rows rather than fifty guesses. Rank by score and tell the user which three to approach first and why — usually the intersection of warm access and strongest evidence match, so their first conversations are their best ones.

Keep one honest column: **the reason this might not work.** A user who knows the weak point in their own case handles the founder's pushback calmly instead of being surprised by it.
