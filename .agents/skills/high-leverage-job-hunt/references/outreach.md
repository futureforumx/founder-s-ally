# Move 3 — Getting to the founder

The target is the founder, or whoever can decide alone. Anyone who has to build consensus for a small experiment will lose interest before they get permission.

## Channels, in rough order of return

- **Warm intro from a mutual connection.** Highest reply rate by a wide margin, because someone else is carrying the credibility.
- **Public conversation first, DM second.** Reply usefully to what they post for two or three weeks before messaging. A DM from a name they recognize isn't cold.
- **Communities and niche groups.** Founder Slacks and Discords, alumni groups, small paid communities, industry groups. Proximity is the whole point.
- **Events.** Low volume, but a five-minute in-person conversation beats twenty DMs.
- **Cold DM or email.** Works, but only with a genuinely specific observation about their business in the first two lines.

## Asking for a warm intro

Make the connector's job trivial. They should be able to forward one paragraph without writing anything themselves, and they need to see that the intro won't embarrass them.

Give them:

- Who the user is, in one line, with the credential that matters here
- Why this specific founder — the observation, not "I'd love to connect"
- What the ask actually is: a conversation about the market, not a job
- A forwardable paragraph they can paste as-is

Draft the message for the user, in their voice, to a named connector. And check whether the connector actually knows the founder well enough — a lukewarm intro from a weak tie is worse than a good cold message, because it spends a relationship for nothing.

## What the first message has to do

One job: earn a call. Not explain the whole opportunity, not sell anything.

It works when it contains something the founder can tell was written for them specifically, and when the ask is small and easy to say yes to. It fails when it reads as a template, when it asks for a job, or when it asks for more than 20 minutes.

Structure that holds up:

1. **Who you are** — one line, the relevant credential only
2. **Why them** — the specific thing you noticed about their business
3. **The frame** — you've been studying this market, you found something that seems to work, you want the view of someone who actually knows the space
4. **The ask** — a short call, framed as you wanting their read, and worth their time because you're bringing what you found

Notice what's absent: no CV, no "I'm looking for opportunities," no attachment, no list of skills. The frame is peer-to-peer research, which happens to be true — the user has done the research.

### Worked example

Good:

> hey Maria — I'm Tom, I built the growth side of [product] (took it from 3k to 60k users, mostly organic short-form).
>
> been digging into the sleep-tech market the last few weeks and something stood out: three of your closest competitors are running creator-led UGC as their main acquisition channel, and from the outside it looks like it's carrying most of their volume. you're clearly not doing it, and I'm curious whether that's deliberate — you'd know things about this market I don't.
>
> any chance of 20 minutes? happy to share what I found either way.

Why it works: a credential with a number, an observation only someone who researched them could make, a question that treats her as the expert, and a small ask that gives her something even if she never works with him.

Bad:

> Hi Maria! I'm a growth marketer with 5+ years of experience in user acquisition, content strategy, and performance marketing. I'm passionate about the wellness space and I think there could be a great fit between my skills and what you're building at [company]. Would you be open to a quick chat about any opportunities on your team? I've attached my CV.

Why it fails: every line is about the sender, nothing is specific to her company, the ask is for a job, and it could have been sent to four hundred people — which she can tell.

## Follow-up

One follow-up, five to seven days later, adding something rather than repeating the ask — a competitor that just launched the play, a number, a short teardown. Then stop. A third message costs more in reputation than the reply is worth, and this market is small enough that the same founders resurface later.

## Volume and pacing

Twenty researched approaches beat four hundred templated ones, and the templated version actively hurts: founders talk to each other, and being known as the person who mass-DMs is expensive. If the user wants scale, scale the *research*, not the sending.

Pace it so the first conversations are with the second-best targets. The top three should get the version of the user that's already had practice.

## Output

Per target, produce: the connector ask (if a warm path exists), the direct message, and the one-line follow-up. Each written from that company's research — if two messages in the set would work interchangeably for two different companies, they're both too generic. Rewrite them.
